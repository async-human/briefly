# Voice and Style Rules

## Headline rules

**Write**: specific, active, concrete
> "Anthropic raises $2.5B at $18.4B valuation, targeting 100B parameter model"
> "OpenAI's GPT-4o mini cuts API costs by 60% for high-volume applications"
> "EU delays AI Act enforcement deadline by six months after industry pressure"

**Never write**:
> "AI company raises funding" *(too vague)*
> "Interesting developments in LLM space" *(zero information)*
> "Report: AI firm may be considering options" *(hedge-words kill specificity)*
> "In this week's AI news..." *(newsletter voice)*

Rules:
- Active voice always. "Google acquires" not "acquisition made by Google".
- Include the most specific number or outcome in the headline when one exists.
- Verbs: acquires, raises, cuts, bans, launches, releases, warns, beats, delays, sues, settles, reverses.
- Max 12 words. If it's longer, cut the least informative phrase.

## Opening lines for why_it_matters_to_you

**Write**:
> "For a founder raising a Series A, this changes your infrastructure cost model before you close."
> "You've been following AI reliability for six weeks — this is the first independent audit that puts numbers on the failure rate."
> "The behavioral shift here (consumers preferring smaller local models) directly contradicts your product's current bet on API-dependent features."

**Never write**:
> "This is worth reading as it touches on your interests."
> "As someone in tech, this affects your field."
> "This could be relevant to your work."
> Any sentence that would apply equally to a random reader.

## Banned phrases (never use these)
- "In this article..."
- "According to..."
- "This piece discusses..."
- "It's worth noting that..."
- "This is an interesting development"
- "Worth a read"
- "One to watch"
- "A must-read for..."
- "Briefly is excited to share..."
- "Today's digest features..."
- "In the fast-moving world of..."

## Tone calibration by role

**Founder**: Direct, tactical, stakes-aware. References fundraising, product, team, burn rate, market timing.

**Engineer**: Precise, technical when warranted. References system design, APIs, latency, costs, open source.

**Investor**: Market-first, pattern-matching, comparative. References valuations, deal flow, competitive dynamics.

**Product manager**: User-outcome oriented. References user research, roadmap implications, competitive signals.

**Researcher**: Rigorous, evidence-focused. References methodology, sample size, replication, prior work.
